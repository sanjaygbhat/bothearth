# BotHearth competitor positioning research — 9 September 2026
Observed date: **9 September 2026**

Produced by a real BotHearth task using Chromium on a temporary Linux VM, then reviewed for publication. The model harness ran on a connected Mac. See the [VM trial and its limitations](REMOTE-VM-TRIAL-2026-09-09.md). This is a bounded market review, not a competitor security audit or an exhaustive inventory. BotHearth evidence below records the facts supplied at task start; consult the [updated engineering review](SECURITY-REVIEW-2026-09-09.md) for subsequent checks.

## Decision
Position BotHearth as **an operator-hosted browser workspace for AI work you can watch and take over**, with unusually clear explanations of storage, model disclosure and authority. This is a defensible product focus, not proof of unique features or superior security.

Self-hosting, model choice, sandboxing, permission gates and visible execution already occur elsewhere. The opportunity is to combine understandable operation with precise, demonstrable boundaries for a technical single operator. Avoid competing on enterprise governance, effortless setup or universal automation reliability.

## Scope and evidence standard
This scoped landscape covers **14 players/project families**, including 12 starting sites and two projects discovered through official links. **26 distinct primary pages were examined for substantive page text**, plus one empty homepage attempt. All browsing occurred through BotHearth Chromium on the attached VM, read using browser_snapshot with interactive_only=false. No independent search service, login, installation, executable download or shell was used.

“Current” below means offered or documented on the observed public page, not independently tested. Competitor marketing, documentation and repository READMEs are publisher evidence; reading them does not verify implementation. No competitor code, certifications, performance benchmarks or contractual deployment terms were independently audited. BotHearth implementation statements come exclusively from the supplied review, separately identified below.

## BotHearth evidence: supplied review versus this research
The following are **implementation facts supplied to the research harness**, not findings from an independent code audit:

- Source-available alpha. Self-hosted daemon; primary task/workspace/profile storage lives on the operator's Mac/Linux host or Linux VM.
- Browser and optional shell use separate non-root containers with resource limits, read-only root filesystems and proxy egress controls. **Native Codex/Claude model CLIs run on the host with their own privileges outside those container restrictions.**
- Operator chooses their own model account through native CLIs or supported API adapters. Providers receive submitted prompts/tool results and potentially screenshots. Self-hosting is not zero egress.
- UI supports watching work, approving new domains and detected sensitive effects, pause/cancel and human takeover. **During acknowledged HUMAN control, model tools cannot capture the browser or act. Returning control resumes visibility.** Detectors cannot guarantee every external effect is caught.
- Profiles are not encrypted by BotHearth; there is no general automatic retention purge. Single operator; paired devices have full operator authority, not enterprise RBAC or tenant separation.
- No independent audit/certification. Internal engineering review fixed four issues; nine Linux container integration tests and 21 network probes passed on commit **629b569**. These are scoped internal results, not proof of broad security, all-platform parity or production reliability.
- No mandatory licence activation or installation reporting. Local-source and prepared-host SSH installation require technical setup.
- Offer: one free perpetual business bot per organization; additional business bots **US$49 once**. Model usage and hosting extra. Outputs may be sold. Standard grants do not permit resale or hosted replication of the software, including modified copies. Use **source-available**, not an unrestricted open-source claim.

This task directly observed approval-pending navigation responses and successful read-only browsing/file output. It did not exercise human takeover, sensitive-effect detection, storage encryption, container isolation or the internal test suite.

## Overlap, realistic gaps and strongest differentiation
**Overlap is substantial.** OpenClaw, Browser Use, Skyvern, Steel and Kortix advertise self-hosting. Open Interpreter, Kortix and OpenHands advertise model/agent choice. Claude has site permissions; Browserbase has interactive takeover; OpenAI describes sensitive-action approvals. “Your own model,” “watch the work,” and “approve domains” are useful benefits but not unique category claims.

**The strongest defensible combination:** operator-hosted primary records + a supervised browser workflow + a precisely described acknowledged-human visibility boundary + clear provider/host privilege disclosures + a single-operator commercial offer. Sell predictability and legibility. No evidence here establishes that this combination is exclusive or more secure than every competitor.

**Likely good fit:** technically capable solo operators, consultants and small-business operators who want a dedicated host/VM and inspectable browser tasks. Begin with public-source research, competitor monitoring, authorized portal extraction and draft preparation with human review. Do not imply every business employee can share one instance safely.

**Gaps:** technical installation; alpha maturity; no BotHearth profile encryption or general retention purge; native-CLI host privileges; incomplete effect detection; no independent assurance or enterprise RBAC. Specialist vendors advertise workflow builders, fleet scale and integrations; this research did not establish equivalent BotHearth breadth. Open-source alternatives may offer broader redistribution rights than BotHearth's standard grants. These are product-selection tradeoffs, not reasons to invent performance comparisons.

## Claims to avoid
- “Only self-hosted/private browser agent,” “most secure,” “zero data leaves,” “100% private,” or “full control over every action.”
- “All execution is container-sandboxed”: host-native model CLIs are outside that boundary.
- “Encrypted profiles,” “automatic deletion,” “enterprise multi-tenancy/RBAC,” or “independently certified/audited.”
- “Human mode hides everything forever”: it blocks model browser capture/action after acknowledgment; visibility resumes on return.
- “One-click setup,” “free forever to run,” or “$49 all-in”: setup is technical and providers/hosting cost extra.
- “Open source with unrestricted resale”; or implying output resale permission permits hosted software replication.
- Competitor rankings, compliance equivalence, customer endorsements, funding totals or prices based on unverified summaries. Vendor badges and internal benchmarks are not independent comparative evidence.
