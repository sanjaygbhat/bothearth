Where Grok Bot and Muse are stronger in public copy

Grok Bot and Muse advertise a 24/7 cloud computer that keeps working when your laptop or phone is away. BotHearth does not sell that service. If you need unattended work, you operate the host (including a VM) yourself, and sleep still stops a local Mac or Linux session.

Grok Bot’s security FAQ is the page that currently carries an ISO statement for the Bot product’s vendor scope. Muse’s checked pages did not. BotHearth’s security page does not claim a certification.

Both competitors ship consumer apps: Grok Bot on desktop and phones; Muse on phones, the web, and WhatsApp at US launch. BotHearth’s documented path remains a source installation.

Those are real selection reasons for buyers who want a hosted teammate, a vendor FAQ with ISO language, or a downloadable consumer client. They are not a score against BotHearth’s custody and control story.

What this page does not claim

It does not rank BotHearth above Grok Bot or Muse on security. It does not treat a FAQ badge as proof of how either vendor’s production systems behave. It does not turn “Not found” into a missing feature. It does not claim that self-hosting keeps model-visible context on your machine.

BotHearth is a source-installed alpha. Connect a supported model account; provider terms, usage limits, and charges still apply. Read security and privacy, the FAQ, and install from source before relying on a task.
